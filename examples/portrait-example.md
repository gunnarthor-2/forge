# Example portrait

*For the Forge. This shows the **shape and depth** of a finished portrait. **Never copy its content**: not the names, the events or the details. Every character you build gets their own. This example was written to show the form only and is not part of the campaign.*

---

**Wenna Strake** — *Human · Ranger · the band's eyes and its long arm*

**Where she comes from.** Wenna grew up on a ferry: her mother's flat-bottomed boat, poled across a wide brown river forty times a day. She learned to read the water before she could read words. When she was fourteen, a drowned thing came up under the ferry at dusk, all teeth and river-weed, and took the boat, the cargo and her mother's left hand. Her mother lived. The ferry didn't. All that came ashore was the ferry's brass bell, cracked down one side, and Wenna wears it tied to her quiver. The man who'd been chasing her mother for the boat's debts came the next week anyway: Holt Varrow, a moneylender with soft gloves and a long ledger. Wenna went to work for coin to pay him, and she has paid him for nine years.

**Who she is in a room.** Quiet, quick to laugh once she knows you, and the first to notice that the innkeeper is lying about the stables. She loves the hunt more than the kill: the hours of tracks and broken reeds, and the moment it all adds up. She fights for her mother, who still lives in a rented room by the river and pretends she isn't waiting for the boat to come back. Her mother is the one thing Wenna is afraid to lose. **She is counting down Holt Varrow's ledger: forty-one silver left, and then her mother's name comes off his page for good.** She won't take a contract against anyone who can't fight back, and she has walked out of two for it. Her crack is pride: she won't accept help with the debt, not from her mother, and not from her one true friend, Pell the bargeman, who keeps offering.

**What she does when the swords come out.** She marks one target with *Hunt Prey* and puts arrows into it from wherever the ground is best, two at a time with *Hunted Shot*, and her *Outwit* edge keeps her a step ahead of whatever she's marked. When something gets close, she steps back and keeps shooting. And there's the thing nobody can explain: on the river, the drowned thing didn't take her. It looked at her, and it let her go.

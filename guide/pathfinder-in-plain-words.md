# Pathfinder in plain words

*For the Forge. Here is how to explain Pathfinder Second Edition (Remaster) to someone who has never played. Use these explanations and put them in your own words. They are only explanations: every number and rule still comes from Archives of Nethys, read live.*

---

## The five pieces of a character (for the opening)

- **Ancestry**: the people you're born to (human, dwarf, elf, orc…). It sets your size, your speed, some of your Hit Points and a few natural talents.
- **Background**: what you did before you took up the sword (a farmhand, a sailor, a temple servant…). It gives you a little training and one useful trick.
- **Class**: how you fight and what you're best at. This is the biggest choice.
- **Attributes**: your six raw strengths, such as how strong, quick or clever you are.
- **Skills and feats**: your training (climbing, lying, healing…) and your special tricks.

## Words to explain the first time they come up

- **Level.** How experienced a character is. Ours start at **level 3**: a few years into the trade and already good at it, which is why there are more choices than at level 1.
- **Heritage.** A smaller choice inside your ancestry, such as which branch of the family or a gift in the blood.
- **Attributes.** There are six: **Strength** (hitting and lifting), **Dexterity** (quickness, aim, dodging), **Constitution** (toughness, how much punishment you can take), **Intelligence** (learning, memory, reasoning), **Wisdom** (noticing things, willpower, instinct) and **Charisma** (presence, persuasion, force of personality). Each one is a number added to your rolls: 0 is average, +4 is the best a starting character gets, and −1 is a weakness.
- **Boosts.** Raise an attribute by one. Your ancestry, background and class each give you boosts, and you get four free ones. Some ancestries also have a **flaw**, which lowers one attribute.
- **Key attribute.** The one attribute your class depends on most. It should usually be your highest.
- **Proficiency.** How well trained you are at something. The ranks are **untrained**, **trained**, **expert**, **master** and **legendary**. Being trained or better adds your level plus a bonus to the roll, which is why training matters so much.
- **Skills.** The things you're trained in, such as Athletics, Stealth, Diplomacy, Medicine or Thievery. A **Lore** skill is narrow knowledge of one subject, for example Mercenary Lore or Farming Lore.
- **Feats.** Special tricks you choose. There are **ancestry feats** (talents of your people), **class feats** (fighting tricks), **skill feats** (clever uses of a skill) and **general feats** (anything else useful).
- **Hit Points (HP).** How much harm you can take before you go down.
- **Armor Class (AC).** How hard you are to hit.
- **Saves.** Your resistance to danger. **Fortitude** resists poison and disease, **Reflex** dodges blasts and traps, and **Will** resists fear and mind-tricks.
- **Perception.** How well you notice things. It is also usually what you roll to act first in a fight.
- **Strike.** An attack with a weapon or a fist.

## How a turn works

On your turn you get **three actions** and one **reaction**.
- An **action** is something like moving, striking, raising a shield or drawing a weapon.
- Some things cost two or three actions, such as most spells.
- Each extra attack in the same turn is harder to land (the **multiple attack penalty**). That's why good players often do one or two attacks and something clever with the third action.
- A **reaction** is something you do out of turn when a trigger happens, such as a counter-attack when an enemy walks past.

This is the best way to explain what a class *feels like* to play: *"On your turn you'd usually spend your three actions like this…"*

## Magic

- Spellcasters draw on one of four **traditions**. **Arcane** is learned magic of formulas and study. **Divine** comes from faith and gods. **Occult** is strange magic of the mind and the unexplained. **Primal** is the magic of nature and the living world.
- **Cantrips** are small spells you can cast as often as you like.
- **Spell slots** are bigger spells you can cast a few times a day.
- **Focus spells** are special spells from your class, recharged by a short rest.
- Some classes are full casters, some mix a little magic into fighting, and some have none at all. All three are good choices.

## Telling them what level 3 means for their choices

A level-3 character is built at level 1 and then levelled up twice. In general, a character gets:
- an ancestry feat at level 1
- class feats at level 1 (most classes) and level 2
- a skill feat at level 2, plus the one from their background
- a general feat and a **skill increase** (more training) at level 3
- extra Hit Points every level

The class's own advancement table on Archives of Nethys is the real list. Read it and follow it.

## When the player asks "what's best?"

Be honest. In Pathfinder, a character who does one thing very well and has a plan for their third action is usually strong. Most choices are close in power, and the one that fits the person is almost always the right one. If a choice would make the character weak at what they want to do, say so plainly and suggest an alternative.
